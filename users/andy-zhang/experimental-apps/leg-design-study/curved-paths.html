The ratio bends the path

Same 300/300 mm links, same q start=100 degrees, same 325 mm vertical stroke.

With t=q-pi and k=r-1, the original phase-zero model is x=L2 cos(t)-L3 cos(k t); z=L2 sin(t)+L3 sin(k t). Equal links at r=2 move vertically. Reducing r below 2 allows sideways motion as the leg compresses; increasing r above 2 bends the normal path the other way.

Speed assumes 0.25 m/s constant vertical movement. Side-load peak is a robust envelope for either sign of Fx with |Fx/Fz|<=0.3, an explicitly assumed sensitivity. |tau_hip|=|Fx Jx+Fz Jz|. A curve changes both force projection and required joint speed; it does not provide free energy.

For the relative knee coordinate, tau_knee=-tau_hip/r and knee speed=-r hip speed. The factor one-half applies only at r=2. Any motor gearbox is a separate mapping. 04 / CURVED LATERAL PATH STUDY

Allowed movement is not stiffness

A single guided joint permits coupled horizontal and vertical motion along the local tangent.

The slope Jx/Jz measures horizontal movement per unit vertical movement. Larger lateral excursion allows the wheel centre to move sideways as the leg compresses, but does not establish a softer suspension. A locked actuator can remain stiff despite a curved path.

J=[Jx,Jz] (metres/radian)
C = J J^T / K_eff
Cxx=Jx^2/K_eff; Cxz=Jx Jz/K_eff; Czz=Jz^2/K_eff

This local one-DOF compliance matrix applies only for a stable effective joint stiffness K_eff in Nm/rad, with the permitted coupled motion free to occur. It has rank one. K_eff must include spring/controller stiffness and load-dependent geometric stiffness. Without those inputs, an absolute horizontal stiffness or spring constant would be invented.

If vertical motion is blocked by rigid contact or another constraint, the coupled sideways motion can also be blocked. Ground contact, traction, tire deformation and wheel rotation decide whether the path is helpful compliance, rolling movement or scrub. Existing wheel-spin cancellation is not a rolling constraint. At the phase-zero fold Jx=0, so the permitted sideways motion vanishes locally even though vertical inversion remains regular.

Strict phase-zero symmetry gives x(-t)=x(t), z(-t)=-z(t). The full path moves sideways toward the fold and returns on the other branch, so full endpoint horizontal drift is zero despite a substantial lateral excursion. Lateral excursion, signed drift and turning points are therefore reported separately.

All compact examples pass the ideal height-inverse check through inversion, but every near-equal coplanar full sweep fails clearance: at the fold |C|=|L2-L3|. The larger 280/285/295/300 mm alternatives clear the normal branch only. Measured axial wheel/chassis and belt geometry is still required. Historical dual-rope remains IMPOSSIBLE /rejected.

Effective ROM solves height roots: qlow=z^-1(zstart+20), qhigh=z^-1(zend-20). The 190 mm, 9:5 case has total q=100-174.484 deg and useful q=109.294-171.110 deg, giving 61.816 deg hip ROM. The 100/175 degree limits are study assumptions, not hardware stops. Separate 90/95 degree start sensitivities expand the full envelope consistently and can permit still shorter links.

No weighted blended score is used. Force leaders sometimes sit near a lateral tolerance edge; rounded-ratio examples are chosen for understandable comparisons. Finite grid and pool retention do not prove global or manufacturing optima.

Start with 190/190 mm and guide 9:5 for the 50 mm case if compact geometry and unresolved axial packaging are acceptable study assumptions. Use the 285 mm, r=1.85 normal-clearance alternative if a coplanar wheel/hip layout is mandatory. Compare the 100 mm case only if more lateral sweep is worth its added rolling/scrub and side-load coupling.

Next specify actual axial/chassis clearances, spring route, controller stiffness, contact conditions and motor inertia/duty before selecting stiffness or motor ratings.
