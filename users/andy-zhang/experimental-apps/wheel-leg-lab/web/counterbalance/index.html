Why the ideal lift is constant

O=(0,0), A=(0,H), B=R(cos θ,sin θ), C=L(cos θ,sin θ)
d²=H²+R²−2HR sin θ
Fspring=k d → τspring=kHR cos θ
Fequivalent lift=τspring/(L cos θ)=kHR/L
τweight=g(mpayloadL+mleverL/2)cos θ

The flat quantity is equivalent upward force at C. Spring tension and spring torque vary with angle. At cos θ=0, the force conversion is singular; the ideal algebraic limiting value does not make the lever able to lift through that configuration.

A real coil can have a nonzero physical free length. The ideal zero-effective-length law needs suitable preload or routing to produce k·d. The ordinary coil comparison uses a nonzero effective free length and loses the constant-lift property.

Use the principle in the wheel-leg suspension

This lever demonstrates the constant-lift law. The wheel-leg model replaces the original lower-link strut with one spring/damper between upper link r₁ and a chassis mount rigidly attached to J1. It retains the floating chassis, 200 mm wheel and 2:1 hip/knee guide.

Tune the replacement unit's spring law, geometry and damping in the wheel-leg controls. Exact gravity balance has neutral elastic ride-height stiffness: damping resists motion without establishing a unique rest height. The separate ride strut remains an optional experiment.
