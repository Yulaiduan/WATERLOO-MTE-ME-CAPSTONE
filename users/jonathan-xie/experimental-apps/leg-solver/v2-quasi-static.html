V2 model, force directions & equilibrium equations

Global headline maxima: the second row searches all θ₂ × β combinations within their master bounds, with α fixed at its current value and the driving force components held fixed. Torque peaks retain their signs; d_z shows the largest travel magnitude. Each card reports a peak pose. Beta sinusoid extrema are evaluated analytically; a bounded θ₂ search targets a remaining magnitude gap of 0.001 in the displayed unit. If the search limit is reached, an estimate and its remaining gap are shown. Hover for signed travel and search details. Maxima recalculate when α, relevant geometry/loads or search bounds change; angle playback within the searched θ₂/β ranges reuses the result.

Point names: A = hip, B = knee, C = wheel centre / tibia endpoint, P = contact patch. V1's old endpoint P is renamed C in v2. Displacements, h, γ and the bottom kinematic plots track C relative to Cᵉ. Both C and P paths are drawn; the contact path uses the current fixed β for its θ₂ sweep.

P_global = C_global − r_w [sin β, cos β]
n̂_global = [sin β, cos β]; t̂_global = [cos β, −sin β]
F_global = F_n n̂ + F_t t̂; M_w = r_w F_t / 1000 (Nm)

β is clockwise from global −z toward the front (−y). Positive F_n points inward; signed F_t is clockwise from the inward radial direction, and positive F_t gives a CCW wheel moment. The prescribed forces are external inputs; they are not automatically recomputed from m₁. Global x / w is out of plane. Positive motor torque is CCW. Displayed τ₂ and τ₄ are motor torques applied to their rotors/links to resist the specified loading.

Driving force convention: select either global F_y/F_z or wheel F_n/F_t. The selected column holds its components fixed while β changes; the other pair updates by F_y = F_n sin β + F_t cos β and F_z = F_n cos β − F_t sin β. Switching preserves the current force vector. All components are signed; a negative F_n means an outward prescribed load, without enforcing unilateral contact.

Height plots: the loading d_z row follows the working θ₂ range, normally from 0 to h_z, extending the axis for any interior extrema. Angles versus d_z covers the full θ₂ = 90–180° travel, including negative values, with 0/h_z boundaries and darker working-range branches. These are parametric curves: multiple θ₂ poses at the same d_z remain separate. Domains and hover inverses are sampled; at zero travel the axis is padded ±0.5 mm for readability.

Gravity and inertia: gravity is −z, with editable g = 9.81 m/s². m₁ is at fixed A; m₂/m₃ are at rod midpoints; m₅ is at B and m₆ at C. m_T = m₁+m₂+m₃+m₅+m₆. m₁ contributes to total chassis support but not τ₂, because A has no virtual displacement. Set masses to zero or g to zero to ignore gravity. Rod I₂ = m₂ L₂²/12 and I₃ = m₃ L₃²/12, thin-rim I₅ = m₅ r₅,I² and I₆ = m₆ r_w² use metres and kg; r₅,I is an explicit effective radius for the compound pulley, since its mass distribution between d/e is unspecified. These inertias are recorded only; no acceleration or dynamic term is applied.

Belt span names: U/L are fixed carrier-side labels, upper/lower in the standard folded pose. For a→b and c→d, U is on the CCW normal side of the carrier. For e→f, U is on its CW normal side. They rotate with the links and are not renamed when global height ordering changes. Forces are applied at exact external tangent points, not centreline approximations. Three stages occupy separate axial planes.

Δ_ab = T_ab,U − T_ab,L; Δ_cd = T_cd,U − T_cd,L; Δ_ef = T_ef,U − T_ef,L
T_i,U = T_i,0 + max(Δ_i, 0)
T_i,L = T_i,0 + max(−Δ_i, 0)
Δ_ef = r_w F_t / r_f
Δ_cd = −(r_e / r_d) Δ_ef
Δ_ab = −[(C−B)×F_w + ((C−B)/2)×W₃] / r_b

The preload rule is the requested fixed-slack-side model, not an elastic belt redistribution law. Plotted tension is the tight span's total T₀ + |Δ|, with U solid / L dotted. The detailed FBD tables show both span tensions. Preload increases shaft reactions; in this ideal model it does not change the required motor torques.

F_w = F_contact + W₆ + F_ef,on6
F_32 = F_w + W₃ + F_ab,on3
F_25 = −(F_cd,on5 + F_ef,on5 + W₅)
F_12 = −(F_32 − F_25 + W₂); F_14 = −F_cd,on4
τ₂ = −[B×(F_32−F_25) + (B/2)×W₂] / 1000
τ₄ = r_c Δ_cd / 1000 = −q_cd q_ef M_w

Positions in the cross products above are in mm, forces in N. F_ij means force from body i on body j: F_32 acts on Link 2; Link 3 receives F_23 = −F_32. F_w is wheel-on-tibia at C; the wheel receives F_36 = −F_w. Likewise Link 2 receives F_52 = −F_25. Body 4's support goes directly to the chassis. FBD 2–5 axes are local u horizontal / v vertical; FBD 6 uses global y horizontal / z vertical. Direction ρ is CCW from the horizontal axis of that FBD (u or y), modulo 360°; undefined at zero force. θ values remain local unwrapped kinematic angles.

Independent motor check: with K = 1 − q_ab + q_ef(q_ab−q_cd), q = θ₂ in radians and R_i the moving mass-centre positions in metres, τ₂ = −[F·∂C/∂q + Σ W_i·∂R_i/∂q + M_w K]. τ₄ = −M_w q_cd q_ef. Each FBD also reports ΣF and ΣM residuals. A separate whole-system support force/couple at A balances the grounded chassis, including m₁.

Each graph varies only its x-axis variable, holding the other inputs at their current values. θ₂ spans 90–180°, β 0–90°, α 0–45°. Dotted range boundaries/shading follow endpoint or master-window settings. Only one master auto-plays; the cycle period controls illustrative parameter motion and has no physical speed/inertial meaning. Graphs update at a limited redraw rate; cursor/FBD values use the current state. Curves are finite-sampled, so narrow features at extreme ratios may be missed.

γ_uv = atan2(d_u,d_v), γ_yz = atan2(d_y,d_z), in degrees (−180° to +180°). At zero displacement both are undefined (—). Curves break at undefined points and ±180° wraps. V1 and V2 use the same kinematic geometry, three open nonslipping belt constraints and extension reference. θ₄ remains locked at 0° while its holding torque is solved.

Scope: equilibrium at a prescribed pose and prescribed contact force, not prediction of motion, unilateral contact, friction feasibility, suspension compliance, passive spring response, impacts, structural strength or manufacturing compatibility. Zero preload is an ideal limiting case. No force is inferred from a mass unless it is an explicit gravity weight. Geometry with overlapping pitch circles is rejected for statics. Arrow lengths/opacity grow monotonically with magnitude and saturate for legibility; numeric tables remain exact.

Inherited v1 equations (legacy point names)

Confirmed arrangement: a is fixed to the chassis; b is rigidly attached to the tibia; c is the locked drive input; d and e are one rigid, freely bearing-mounted compound pulley at B; f is fixed to the wheel. All three belts are open, taut and nonslipping. Ratios use pitch radii: q₂₃ = L₂/L₃, q_ab = r_a/r_b, q_cd = r_c/r_d, q_ef = r_e/r_f.

Angle directions: θ₂ points B→A and θ₃ points P→B, measured counterclockwise from +u. This makes θ₂ = 90° the downward vertical femur and θ₂ = 180° the rightward horizontal femur. The distal vectors A→B and B→P point 180° away. γ₂ = 180° − θ₂ is the acute reference angle of the reversed femur vector; it is not the absolute orientation of A→B.

θ₃ = φ₀ + (180° − θ₂)(q_ab − 1)
B = −L₂ [cos θ₂, sin θ₂]
P = B − L₃ [cos θ₃, sin θ₃]
Δθ₂ = θ₂ − θ₂ᵉ; Δθ₃ = (1 − q_ab) Δθ₂
θ₄ = 0; θ₅ = (1 − q_cd) Δθ₂
θ₆ = q_ef θ₅ + (1 − q_ef) Δθ₃

The open-belt constraint is written in each rotating carrier frame: r_a(Δθ₁ − Δθ₂) = r_b(Δθ₃ − Δθ₂), r_c(Δθ₄ − Δθ₂) = r_d(Δθ₅ − Δθ₂), and r_e(Δθ₅ − Δθ₃) = r_f(Δθ₆ − Δθ₃). The relative knee angle changes by −q_ab Δθ₂; the tibia’s absolute angle changes by (1 − q_ab) Δθ₂.

[d_u, d_v] = P(θ₂) − P(θ₂ᵉ)
d_y = cos α · d_u + sin α · d_v
d_z = −sin α · d_u + cos α · d_v
h_i = d_i(θ₂ᶜ)

The local axes rotate clockwise by α relative to the global y,z axes. The shared out-of-plane axis is w (also global x); all out-of-plane displacement is zero. θ₄ = 0 means a fixed local orientation; its drawn global orientation is −α.

Straight-path check: at L₂ = L₃ = L, q_ab = 2, φ₀ = 0: P_u = 0 and P_v = −2L sin θ₂. Nonzero φ₀ generally changes the path. For arbitrary parameters, labels “extension” and “compression” identify your chosen endpoints; they do not guarantee monotonic vertical travel. The table reports interior displacement extrema separately from h.

Displacement angles: γ_uv = atan2(d_u, d_v), γ_yz = atan2(d_y, d_z), in degrees (−180° to +180°). Both are undefined at zero displacement and shown as —; CSV leaves those cells empty. These are travel-direction angles, not link rotations.

Visualization: link lengths, wheel and pulley pitch radii share one scale. Belt paths use external common tangents and circular wrap arcs. Belt dots represent equally spaced material points using carrier-relative pulley rotation; pulley dots are rigid material markers. Different dot counts are not matched tooth pitches. Projected overlaps between different belt stages do not imply physical contact because the stages are in separate axial planes.

Scope: analytical planar kinematics only. No wheel/ground rolling constraint, belt stretch, backlash, compliance, friction, spring, force, mass, torque or collision solver. Wheel radius affects the drawing and the free wheel rim-arc readout; it does not affect θ₆ or the wheel-centre path. Pulley overlap within a belt stage is flagged. Tooth count, wrap adequacy and 3D packaging remain design checks. The full graph domain is 90–180°; the animation uses only your working range. Dotted markers show signed endpoint settings.
