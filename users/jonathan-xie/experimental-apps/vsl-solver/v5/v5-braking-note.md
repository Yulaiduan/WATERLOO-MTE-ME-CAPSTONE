# Hip braking, wheel braking and the final holding switch

The fall calculation brakes **leg compression with the hip actuator τ₂**.
It does not need a horizontal wheel force, nonzero β, chassis tilt, or a wheel
braking torque τ₄. In this model Fy=0 and β=0; therefore the contact force has
no moment about the wheel centre and τ₄=0. A nonzero α changes the vertical
mechanical advantage but is not the reason that hip braking works. A vertical
global Fz has components along the tilted local u/v axes; that does not create a
nonzero global Fy in this calculation.

Let q=θ₂ in radians, x=d_z in metres, and J=dx/dq>0 during compression.
The hip motor applies τ₂=−T, opposing increasing q. It extracts energy

    W_motor = −∫ T dq = −T(q−q_e).

This is why braking works even for a perfectly vertical leg trajectory with
α=0, Fy=0 and no wheel rotation. The vertical load still has a generalized
moment Fz·J about the leg's degree of freedom. With physical spring torque τS,

    τ₂ + τS + Fz J = 0,
    Fz = (T−τS)/J,
    a_z/g = Fz/(mT g) − 1.

β=0 removes wheel-centre contact torque; it does not remove the hip's vertical
load lever arm J. Ground contact itself does no work at an ideal stationary
contact point: energy is stored in the spring or absorbed/regenerated by the
hip motor as the chassis descends and the leg compresses.

## Why first rest does not mean zero braking acceleration

The constant torque T is selected so that kinetic energy reaches zero at q_c:

    T = [mT g(h+D) − ΔU_s] / (q_c−q_e).

This energy equation specifies the endpoint velocity. It does not also enforce
a_z=0 there. Immediately before first rest, the braking force can exceed weight.
Continuing the same torque after that instant would initiate upward motion rather
than hold the chassis still. The controller must switch to

    Fz_hold = mT g,
    a_z,hold = 0,
    τ₂,hold = −mT g J_c − τS,c.

The difference between holding and braking torque is

    τ₂,hold − (−T) = mT a_z,brake J_c.

The plots show this ideal instantaneous change as a dashed vertical step at the
same θ₂ and the same time, with a hollow holding marker. It is a control switch,
not an extra compression distance or a smooth acceleration ramp. The sweep plots
report peaks during the braking stroke; the appended holding marker is excluded
from those peaks. The fall-mode force diagrams retain the braking left limit at
θ₂c, so the appended right-limit marker is explicitly identified as holding.

## Default V5 example, with α=β=0

For mT=8.75kg and h=0.25m, using the default spring/leg geometry:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---:|
| Constant hip braking torque τ₂ | −18.91156 Nm |
| Wheel drive/braking torque τ₄ | 0 Nm |
| Time from touchdown to first rest | 0.466385 s |
| Final velocity | 0 m/s |
| Net acceleration immediately before switching | +0.381411 g |
| Ground force immediately before switching | 1.381411 mTg |
| Net acceleration after switching to holding | 0 g |
| Ground force after switching to holding | 1 mTg |

Thus the effect exists at zero α, with zero horizontal force. It comes from
hip torque doing negative work during vertical compression, not from balancing
a horizontal force or braking the wheel.
