# VSL V5 fall recovery model

Author: Jonathan Xie with Codex. Date: 2026-10-24. Experimental numerical model;
analytical consistency does not establish a safe physical drop height or actuator rating.

## Scope and coordinates

V5 retains the V4 kinematics, spring geometry, static FBDs, plots and spring
optimizer. Analysis Mode selects either the original prescribed-load calculation
or fall-derived loads. The fall study ignores prescribed Fy/Fz/Fn/Ft,
uses beta=0, and assigns the full total mass m=sum(m1,m2,m3,m5,m6) to a vertical
translating point. Moving-link kinetic energy is excluded by user instruction.
A mirrored leg holds pitch and balances horizontal forces, without halving m
in this single-leg computation. Alpha is fixed during each catch. This is not
a multibody/contact-impact model or a critically damped response.

Geometry inputs use mm/deg; the equations below use metres and radians.
q=theta2 increases from qe=theta2e to qc=theta2c. Define
x(q)=dz(q)/1000, D=x(qc), J=dx/dq. The supported chassis moves downward by x;
its signed global upward velocity is vz=-xdot and acceleration az=-xddot.
The study requires J>0 throughout the working interval. It rejects travel across
a vertical dead centre or a reversal rather than inventing a single-valued time.

## Impact and constant braking torque

The leg stays fully extended during free fall. From rest over height h,

    vz(0) = -sqrt(2 g h),   E0 = 0.5 m vz(0)^2 = m g h.

Time zero in all study plots is first ground contact, not release. The foot is
assumed massless and to remain on the ground without a chassis velocity jump.
No independent impact impulse, tyre compliance, restitution or air drag is modeled.

Let Us(q) be the existing V4 tension-spring energy. It includes changing tangent
span and wrap for finite rs, including the tension-only/slack law. With motor
torque tau2=-T constant during positive q motion,

    K(q) = E0 + m g x(q) - [Us(q)-Us(qe)] - T (q-qe).

Rest at qc gives the energy torque

    T = [m g (h+D) - (Us(qc)-Us(qe))] / (qc-qe).

This accounts for the additional gravitational potential lost during compression;
using mgh alone would omit mgD. The spring stores energy; the motor extracts work
T(qc-qe), which must be dissipated or regenerated by a real actuator.

For any torque schedule with |tau2(q)|<=M, its extracted motor work cannot exceed
M(qc-qe). Therefore M>=T when positive braking work is required. Constant tau2=-T
attains this lower bound if its entire path is feasible. This proves minimum peak
magnitude for that regime, with no motor speed/power/response constraints.
If the constant trajectory stops early, the energy bound alone is not achievable
by that trajectory; a larger cap with a different schedule may be required.
V5 reports infeasibility rather than claiming to optimize those schedules.
If T<0, the requested endpoint needs net motor energy injection; this braking-only
study reports that explicitly. It does not silently take an absolute value.

## Force, acceleration, velocity and time

Differentiating spring energy gives dUs/dq=-tauS. Thus

    dK/dq = m g J + tauS - T,
    Fz = (T-tauS)/J,
    az = Fz/m - g,
    az/g = Fz/(m g) - 1,
    vz = -sqrt(2 K/m),
    dt/dq = J/sqrt(2 K/m).

Fz is the upward ground reaction. Negative Fz is impossible for unilateral
ground contact and causes rejection. The equations also follow from
m J^2 qddot + m J J' qdot^2 = m g J + tauS - T; the J' term is not omitted
by using the translational energy method.

K must be positive in the interior and zero at the final endpoint. A finite-time
decelerating endpoint requires K'(qc)<0. At h=0 a finite forward start also
requires K'(qe)>0. Degenerate rest-to-rest cases with no launch energy and no
forward force are rejected. Very small endpoint energies use a scale-aware
roundoff tolerance; materially negative K is never clipped into a valid path.

For time quadrature, q=qe+(qc-qe) sin^2(pi eta/2) removes the square-root endpoint
singularities. Composite Simpson integration uses the analytical endpoint limits.
The current curve uses 800 intervals; each of 61 alpha samples uses 240 intervals;
each of 51 height samples uses 160 intervals. The interactive load adapter caches
80-interval trajectories and replaces the current one with the full study result.
Arbitrary pose force/energy values are evaluated directly, with time interpolated.
Fall-mode headline peaks scan 321 poses and are labelled sampled peaks.
Peaks and feasibility are sampled, not a certified continuous global bound.

## Plot interpretation

The 5×4 grid shows signed tau2, signed net upward az/g with Fz on the right axis,
signed vz (downward negative), and dz in mm. Columns are q, elapsed contact time,
peak magnitude versus the user alpha range and peak magnitude versus the user h
range at current alpha. Every row retains its signal across all four columns; there are no duration
substitutions. The alpha velocity peak is constant for a monotonically decelerating
catch, and the height displacement peak is geometric and constant. Sweep peaks cover
the entire working stroke, rather than only the live theta pose. The displacement
peak is geometric and independent of mass, spring energy and height.

Finite y values set the default axis bounds with padding; constant signals receive
a small finite band. Include-zero expands those bounds to include zero. Absolute
magnitude changes pointwise/time signals; peak columns already show magnitudes.
For alpha/height acceleration peaks whose absolute acceleration extrema are
positive, the right axis uses the exact relation max(Fz)=mg[1+max(az/g)].
If a negative acceleration extremum dominates, maximum |az/g| and maximum Fz
are separate curves with independent left/right scales because their extrema
can occur at different poses. Signed pointwise/time axes use Fz=mg(1+az/g). Absolute pointwise plots with negative net-acceleration segments
show Fz as an independent right-axis curve to avoid an ambiguous affine mapping.
When net acceleration stays nonnegative, the affine right-axis mapping remains exact.
Zero is an explicit labelled guide; including zero makes it a one-sided bound
without discarding data of either sign. Net acceleration additionally labels a
1g guide when it lies inside the displayed range.

The final velocity is exactly zero at qc and explicitly marked on the plots.
Pointwise/time plots append a same-time, same-theta holding state as a dashed
endpoint step and hollow marker: net a_z/g=0, Fz=mg, and
tau2_hold=-mg*J_c-tauS_c. Braking-stroke peak sweeps exclude this holding marker.
The CSV appends the same holding row with an explicit phase column.
The original fall-mode force diagrams retain the braking left limit at qc.
The plotted acceleration is **net acceleration**, not ground force in weight units:
1mg support means az=0, whereas 1g upward net acceleration means Fz=2mg.
The final braking-curve acceleration, before the dashed holding step, is the
left limit under continued constant braking;
it need not be zero when velocity first reaches zero. Holding afterward requires
a torque switch, giving az=0 and Fz=mg. No finite-duration post-stop rebound/holding transient is
simulated; only the ideal instantaneous holding switch is appended. For the default catch the braking-end value is about 0.3814g and the
corresponding load is 1.3814mg. This is a finite-time energy catch, not a critically
damped asymptotic response. If az>=0 throughout, peak |vz| equals sqrt(2gh),
independently of alpha; downward-accelerating segments can exceed it and are flagged.

## Fall-derived analysis mode

In Global and Wheel modes, the old sections retain their prescribed loads and
live beta. Changing h does not affect them. In fall mode, beta=0 and Fy=0 are
fixed; each theta maps to one point on the catch and its instantaneous upward Fz.
The unchanged static FBD solver receives that force, the original spring geometry
and all total mass lumped at the chassis (zero moving-link weight), consistent
with the confirmed point-mass approximation. Gravity/inertia are already reflected
in Fz; neither mg nor spring force is added a second time. The reconstructed
motor torque equals -T along the entire stroke, with tau4=0 for this vertical
contact convention. This is an instantaneous force reconstruction, not an added
full multibody inertial model.

Original torque/force plots and headlines use that reconstruction. Alpha sweeps
solve a new catch for each alpha; beta sweeps remain beta=0. Poses outside the
working catch or infeasible catches produce gaps/unavailable results. Manual
force inputs and beta are preserved for switching back. The spring optimizer
continues to target its original static prescribed-load objective.

## State and persistence

V5 JSON snapshots use general-vsl/v5-fall and additionally carry fallStudy with
heightM, massBounds, heightBounds, analysisMode, includeZero and absolute.
Older V5 states default to manual-load mode with both plot options off. Existing V4 snapshots remain loadable.
The copied workbook starts byte-identical to V4, but the default V5 path and
browser storage keys are separate. Fall columns appear on explicit V5 save.
Fall peak outputs are saved only after a feasible current study completes.
The original V4 folder is never rewritten by V5 creation or normal V5 startup.

See [hip braking versus wheel braking](v5-braking-note.md) for the zero-alpha,
zero-horizontal-force derivation and the endpoint controller switch.

The fifth fall row plots elapsed contact time versus theta2 and time (y=x),
and total catch time versus alpha and fall height using their master bounds.
Time uses seconds and remains available in every Analysis Mode.
