How the feedback loop fits together

Goals become coordinated corner targets. The MCU compares the desired body pose with a valid estimate, then maps corner targets through the leg geometry and coupled wheel transmission. Local motor feedback runs in the selected drive mode; the linked knee has no independent PID.

Measurements close the loop. The chassis IMU supplies attitude and angular rates. Encoders supply leg and wheel state, with local motor feedback at each drive. Height also needs valid support/geometry information. Mode, travel, command-age and torque limits gate commands. This lab calculates the leg impedance and its inherited chassis effect; the complete loop remains a proposed architecture.

Model details, coordinates & limits

One-leg mapping. q is source Link2 θ₂ in radians, positive CCW. h is positive downward hip-to-wheel-centre drop. The drawing uses Jon's global y/z coordinates and the actual source wheel path.

ω = 2π fₙ
kₕ = m_eff ω²    cₕ = 2ζ m_eff ω
h(q) = 0.6 cos(20°) sin(q)
Jₕ(q) = 0.6 cos(20°) cos(q)
Kp = kₕ Jₕ²     Kd = cₕ Jₕ²
Ki = 0

Local zero-error gains assume current-pose static-load/spring compensation. The axial spring rate (6 N/mm in the source) is a separate quantity from kₕ. These are not complete inverse dynamics, measured force tracking or final driver gains.

Wheel coupling remains. DP#3 obeys Δφ_w = −0.25 Δq + 0.25 Δθ₄. Holding wheel angle during articulation requires Δθ₄ = Δq in source coordinates. Wheel feedback introduces cross terms; independent local motor PDs do not automatically reproduce them. Additional motor reduction and vendor conversions are outside this lab.

a = L/2     b = W/2
K_heave = 4kₕ    C_heave = 4cₕ
K_roll = 4kₕb²   C_roll = 4cₕb²
K_pitch = 4kₕa²  C_pitch = 4cₕa²

Direct gain mode uses fₙ = √(kₕ/m_eff)/(2π) and ζ = cₕ/(2√(kₕm_eff)). Zero damping is allowed as an ideal undamped case. Slider bounds are exploration/source limits, not validated hardware limits.
